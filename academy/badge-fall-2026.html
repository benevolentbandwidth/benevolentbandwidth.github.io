GenAI Academy for Nonprofits — Fall 2026

Awarded to people from nonprofit organizations who completed all four live sessions and the hands-on work of our first GenAI Academy cohort.

  • Issued by Benevolent Bandwidth Foundation
  • Open Badges 3.0 credential

About this badge

This badge shows that the earner completed the GenAI Academy for Nonprofits, a free program run by the Benevolent Bandwidth Foundation (B2). The Fall 2026 cohort was the academy's first, a pilot group.

The academy is open only to people who work with nonprofit organizations. It ran as four live, instructor-led online sessions of about two hours each, from October 24 to November 14, 2026.

Earning the badge took more than attendance. Earners completed three hands-on assignments: a prompting lab, an interactive grant tracker, and a data dashboard. All of the work used fictional or synthetic data and followed the academy's rules for responsible AI use.

Earning criteria

To earn this badge, a participant met all four of the following criteria.

  1. Attended all four live sessions

    Participants attended the four live, instructor-led sessions of the Benevolent Bandwidth Foundation’s GenAI Academy for Nonprofits, about two hours each, held October 24 – November 14, 2026:

    • Session 1: AI Foundations, Prompting, and Context Engineering
    • Session 2: Building Useful Tools with AI (Vibe Coding)
    • Session 3: Data Generation and AI-Powered Analysis
    • Session 4: Agentic AI Orchestration with n8n

  2. Structured prompts with a written rationale

    Participants completed a hands-on prompting lab with 18 prompting strategies built around a fictional youth nonprofit.

    Submitted structured prompts defining task, context, constraints, and format, using techniques such as role assignment, few-shot examples, and fenced sources. Included a written explanation of how each prompt was built, why each technique was chosen, and where guardrails were added, such as flagging missing facts and labeling drafts for human review.

  3. Interactive grant tracker

    Vibe-coded a working grant tracker with Claude from 20 fictional grant records and a project brief. Briefed the AI on the user, job, and data; reviewed its plan before building; delivered a readable grant list with status filter, keyword search, and reset; flagged missing information as “not provided” instead of inventing it; and refined the tool through conversational iteration.

  4. Data dashboard

    Built a defensible dashboard from a 144-row synthetic nonprofit dataset using a seven-step prompt pipeline. Set explicit data, calculation, behavior, and boundary rules; had the AI audit the data for duplicates, gaps, and impossible records and flag them rather than fix them; defined KPI formulas before building; then built, verified, and restyled the dashboard.

What earners can do

Earners practiced these skills in the sessions and assignments.

  • Structured prompting: task, context, constraints, and format; zero-shot, few-shot, and role-based patterns
  • Context engineering: grounding AI output in approved sources
  • AI output verification: catching hallucinations, flagging missing or conflicting facts, labeling drafts for human review
  • Responsible AI guardrails: protecting beneficiary data, human approval steps, named ownership, the 4 Ds decision framework
  • Vibe coding: building and refining an interactive web tool through conversation with AI
  • AI-assisted data analysis: auditing data, defining KPIs explicitly, building a defensible dashboard
  • Synthetic data and data privacy: practicing safely, scrubbing PII, disabling model training on your data
  • Agentic workflow design (n8n): how an AI agent uses tools inside a governed workflow with a human approval gate

About the issuer

The Benevolent Bandwidth Foundation (B2) brings together volunteers with technical and nonprofit backgrounds to make AI useful for public benefit.

B2 helps nonprofits in three ways: the GenAI Academy, custom AI solutions built by a distributed volunteer network, and the Agentic Nonprofit platform of ready-made agents, which is in development.

The academy exists so that people working at nonprofits can learn to use AI well and safely, with practice on real nonprofit tasks, at no cost.

How to verify a badge

Every badge is issued through Certifier and has its own credential page. That page shows the earner's name, the issue date, and the issuer, and confirms that the credential is authentic and has not been revoked.

  1. Ask the earner for the credential link they received, or open the credential linked from their LinkedIn profile.
  2. On the credential page, check that the earner's name and issue date are correct and that the issuer is the Benevolent Bandwidth Foundation.
  3. Confirm the page shows the credential as valid.

A badge image on its own is not proof. The credential page is. If you have questions about a badge, email Participant Support & Logistics at kirill@benevolentbandwidth.org.
